Art Basel 2026 Scams: Fake Tickets, Tables and Rentals

By Miami Scammers | November 2026 | Miami, FL

Art Basel Miami Beach is one of the most anticipated events on Miami's calendar — and one of the most exploited. Every December, the week-long art fair brings 80,000+ visitors, $500M+ in art sales, and an entire parallel economy of parties, pop-ups, and satellite events that have nothing to do with the official fair but leverage its name and energy.

That parallel economy is rife with fraud. Here's what to watch for heading into Art Basel 2026 and how to protect yourself.

Fake Event Tickets

The most common Art Basel scam is the fake ticket. Fraudsters create convincing PDFs, QR codes, and confirmation emails for sold-out or high-demand events — parties at Soho House, the Edition, collector dinners, gallery openings — and sell them on Instagram DMs, Telegram, and Facebook Marketplace.

How it works: the buyer pays via Zelle, Venmo, or CashApp, receives what looks like a legitimate ticket, and shows up at the door to find out the ticket is invalid. By then the seller is unreachable.

Red flags: Sellers who reach out unsolicited, payment requests via peer-to-peer apps with no buyer protection, QR codes that can't be verified on the event's official page, prices that seem low for a sold-out event, and event pages that only exist on Instagram Stories (no website, no official venue confirmation).

Protect yourself: Buy tickets only from the official event page or Eventbrite listing. Verify the event exists and is associated with the venue it claims. Never pay via Zelle or CashApp for tickets — use a credit card where you can dispute the charge.

Fraudulent VIP Table Reservations

Miami's Art Basel week nightlife is insane — and table minimums at top venues run $5,000 to $50,000+. Scammers exploit this by posing as promoters with "connections" to bypass waitlists and secure premium tables at clubs, rooftops, and exclusive events.

The scam: they collect a deposit or partial payment upfront to "secure" the table, then either vanish entirely or show up on the night and have no actual reservation. The victim is left without a table and without recourse — often after paying thousands.

We have documented cases of Miami event promoters doing exactly this — collecting deposits from venues and vendors, then failing to show or delivering nothing.

Protect yourself: Contact the venue directly to confirm any reservation before paying. Legitimate table reservations go through the venue's own booking team — not a third-party promoter who can't provide a venue contact. Get everything in writing with a cancellation/refund policy before paying any deposit.

Short-Term Rental Fraud

Art Basel week drives Miami rental rates to absurd levels — $10,000 to $50,000+ per week for South Beach properties. Scammers exploit this by listing properties they don't own or control on VRBO, Airbnb, and especially Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist.

The setup: a beautiful listing at a slightly-below-market rate. The "landlord" or host is enthusiastic, responsive, and professional-looking. They ask for a security deposit or the first month upfront via wire transfer or Zelle to "hold" the property. The victim pays. The host goes dark. The property either doesn't exist as listed, is already booked to other victims, or was never the host's to rent.

Red flags: Wire transfer or Zelle payment requests before signing a lease, listings with no street address, landlords who refuse in-person or video tours of the actual property, prices that seem low for Art Basel week, and hosts who can't answer questions about parking, building rules, or local contact info.

Protect yourself: Use only Airbnb or VRBO with their built-in payment protection and dispute processes. Never pay outside the platform. If booking directly, verify the host's ownership via the Miami-Dade property appraiser's website (miamidade.gov) before transferring any funds. Pay by credit card, never wire/Zelle.

Predatory Promoters & the "Guest List" Scam

Art Basel week is ground zero for the Miami promoter scam. Strangers on the beach, at hotels, and in hotel lobbies approach visitors offering access to exclusive events, gallery parties, and "private collections" — for a fee or in exchange for following them on Instagram.

Beyond the obvious, there's a subtler version: promoters who collect a list of attendees and charge per head for entry, then can't deliver at the door, or who collect payment from multiple venue stakeholders without the actual arrangements in place.

Protect yourself: Verify that the event exists before paying anyone for access. The official Art Basel website (artbasel.com) lists all official events. For satellite events, the venue's own Instagram or website should reference the event. If a promoter can't connect you directly with a venue contact, do not pay.

Quick Reference: Art Basel Scam Checklist

  • Verify ticket purchases through the official event page, never via Instagram DM
  • Contact the venue directly before paying any deposit for a table or event
  • Pay for rentals only through Airbnb / VRBO — never Zelle, CashApp, or wire transfer
  • Verify property ownership at miamidade.gov before any direct rental payment
  • Get all arrangements in writing with a clear refund policy before money changes hands
  • If a deal requires you to decide or pay immediately, that urgency is the scam
