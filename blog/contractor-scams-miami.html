Contractor Scams in Miami: How to Protect Yourself Before You Pay a Dime

📅 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read
Back to Blog

After every hurricane season, Miami and South Florida face a second wave of damage — not from the storm, but from the contractors that follow it. Storm chasers, unlicensed roofers, and renovation scammers descend on affected neighborhoods, collecting insurance money and deposits for work they never complete or complete dangerously. But bad contractors are not just a post-storm problem. Unlicensed tradespeople, deposit thieves, and permit fraudsters operate year-round in Miami-Dade and Broward County.

Florida consistently ranks among the worst states in the country for contractor fraud. The combination of an aging housing stock, frequent storm damage, a large population of retirees, and relatively lax enforcement creates ideal conditions for bad actors. Here's the complete playbook — and how to make sure none of it happens to you.

🚨 The Most Common Contractor Scams in Miami

1. The Storm Chaser

After a hurricane or severe storm, door-to-door contractors flood Miami neighborhoods with offers to assess damage and handle insurance claims. They offer to do the inspection for free, help you file your insurance claim, and get started immediately. What actually happens: they push you to sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) document, which hands them control of your insurance claim. They inflate repair estimates, the insurer disputes the claim, your property sits in legal limbo for months, and you may end up with no repairs and a complicated lawsuit.

2. The Deposit Ghost

This is the most common contractor scam in Miami outside of storm season. A contractor provides a quote, often genuinely competitive, asks for 50 percent or more upfront, starts work (or appears to start), and then disappears. Tools are left at the site, calls go unanswered, and the work — your kitchen, your roof, your bathroom — is left demolished with no one coming back to finish it. Finding a licensed contractor to take over a half-demolished job often costs significantly more than the original project would have.

3. The Unlicensed Contractor

Florida requires contractors to be licensed for virtually all significant construction work. An unlicensed contractor cannot legally pull permits, cannot be bonded, and carries no accountability if the work fails. Work performed by unlicensed contractors is frequently substandard, cannot be inspected by code, and may need to be entirely redone at your expense. When something goes wrong — and it often does — an unlicensed contractor has no license to lose and limited legal accountability.

4. The Permit Fraud

Some contractors are licensed but skip the permit process to save time and avoid inspections. This seems convenient — faster work, less hassle. What it actually creates: unpermitted work that fails to meet code, which can require demolition and reconstruction when you try to sell the property or when it's discovered during an inspection. In Miami-Dade County, you as the property owner are ultimately responsible for unpermitted work on your property, even if a contractor was paid to handle it.

5. The Change Order Trap

The low bid gets the job. Once your home is partially demolished and the contractor is the only logical person to continue the work, the change orders begin. Unexpected structural issues, materials that turned out to be more expensive, labor costs that weren't anticipated — each one is presented as unavoidable and urgent. By the time the project is complete, you've paid two or three times the original estimate, with no recourse because you signed each change order under pressure.

Florida law limits upfront deposits: Under Florida Statutes §489.126, a contractor may not demand more than 10 percent of the contract price as a down payment for a home improvement contract, or $1,000, whichever is less — unless they are licensed and have obtained a construction permit. Any contractor demanding more than this upfront is either unfamiliar with Florida law or deliberately circumventing it. Either way, it's a red flag.

📋 Florida Contractor Licensing Requirements

All significant construction work in Florida requires a licensed contractor. The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) licenses contractors and maintains a public database you can search for free.

  • General Contractors must be licensed for any project over $1,000. Search at myfloridalicense.com under "Contractor."
  • Roofing contractors require a specific roofing license — a general contractor license alone does not cover roofing.
  • Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC each require their own specialized license. Never let an unlicensed tradesperson do work that requires a license.
  • Specialty contractors — tile, drywall, painting, and similar trades — have their own license categories. Verify the specific license type matches the work being performed.

Verify permit history before you hire: Contact the Miami-Dade Building Department at miamidade.gov or call (786) 315-2000 and ask about any contractor's permit history at your address and others. A contractor who has never pulled permits, or has open permits they never closed, is a contractor you don't want. Permit records are public information.

✅ The 10-Step Contractor Vetting Checklist

Before you sign a contract or hand over any money to a contractor in Miami, work through every item on this list:

  1. Verify their DBPR license. Go to myfloridalicense.com, search their name and license number, and confirm the license is active, in the correct category, and not under any disciplinary action.
  2. Check the Better Business Bureau. Search the company name at bbb.org. Look at both the rating and the complaint history — a company can have a decent rating but many resolved complaints, which still tells you something.
  3. Get a minimum of three bids. For any project over $2,000, get at least three written bids. If one bid is dramatically lower than the others, ask why. It's often a sign that corners will be cut or the low bid will balloon with change orders.
  4. Demand a written contract with a payment schedule. The contract should specify: scope of work, materials to be used, timeline, payment milestones tied to work completion, and what happens if the contractor fails to perform.
  5. Never pay cash. Always pay by check or credit card — methods that create a paper trail and, in the case of credit cards, offer dispute protection.
  6. Verify their insurance certificate. Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability coverage and workers' compensation. Call the insurer to confirm the policy is active. An uninsured contractor leaves you liable for accidents on your property.
  7. Search Google reviews and Nextdoor. Read reviews on Google Maps and search their company name on Nextdoor, where local neighbors often share firsthand experiences with contractors.
  8. Search their name on MiamiScammers.com. Check whether they've been reported by prior clients in the Miami area.
  9. Demand a lien waiver upon final payment. A contractor who fails to pay their subcontractors or suppliers can place a mechanic's lien on your property — even if you paid the general contractor in full. A lien waiver protects you from this.
  10. Pay the final balance only after the final inspection passes. Do not make the final payment until all work is complete, all permits are closed, and you've received written confirmation from the building department that inspections passed.

If a Contractor Scammed You in Miami

  • File a complaint with the Florida DBPR at myfloridalicense.com. If the contractor is licensed, a complaint can result in license suspension or revocation — the most effective consequence available to you. File regardless of whether you pursue other remedies.
  • File a consumer fraud complaint with the Florida Attorney General at myfloridalegal.com. Pattern complaints about the same contractor can trigger investigations under Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act.
  • File in Miami-Dade Small Claims Court for disputes up to $8,000. You don't need an attorney. File at the Miami-Dade Clerk of Courts and use a process server to serve the contractor at their registered business address.
  • Contact Miami-Dade Consumer Protection at miamidade.gov/consumer. They accept consumer complaints about contractor fraud and can sometimes facilitate resolution or refer cases to law enforcement.
  • Contact local law enforcement if the contractor took a substantial deposit and disappeared. Theft by unlawful taking over $750 is a third-degree felony in Florida. File a police report and retain all documentation.

Critical insurance risk: Work performed by an unlicensed contractor — or permitted work done without proper permits — can void your homeowner's insurance policy if damage occurs related to that work. If your roof was installed by an unlicensed roofer and leaks cause interior damage, your insurer may deny the claim entirely. The legal liability for injuries on your property that occur during unlicensed work may also fall on you, not the contractor.

Miami's construction market is competitive, and the pressure to move fast and pay upfront is real. But the contractors who push hardest for cash and least for paperwork are almost always the ones who will cost you the most in the end. License verification, permit confirmation, and payment milestones cost nothing — and can save you tens of thousands of dollars.
