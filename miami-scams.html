Common Scams in Miami — The Complete Guide

Miami's fast-moving real estate, nightlife, and business scenes make it fertile ground for fraud. This guide covers every major scam category, the warning signs, and links to documented local cases.

Rental & Real Estate

Rental & Real Estate Fraud

Miami's housing market is one of the most competitive in the country, which makes it a prime target for rental scammers. Fraudsters post listings on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, and Zillow for properties they don't own or control. Victims who pay deposits often find out the property was never available — or already rented to someone else.

Security deposit fraud is also rampant. Some landlords pocket deposits and refuse to return them when tenants vacate, citing phantom damages.

Red flags: Listings priced well below market, landlords who refuse in-person showings, requests for payment via Zelle or CashApp before you've signed a lease, and landlords who can't answer basic questions about the property.

Events & Nightlife

Event & Nightlife Scams

Miami has one of the most active event and nightlife scenes in the world — and one of the most fraud-prone. Fake promoters collect advance payments from venues and vendors, then vanish when the event date arrives. Attendees who pre-paid for tickets are left with nothing.

Labor exploitation is also common: promoters recruit staff with promises of payment that never materializes, particularly targeting undocumented workers who have fewer legal protections.

Red flags: Promoters who ask for deposits before contracts are signed, events promoted heavily on Instagram with no verifiable venue booking, organizers who can't provide venue contact information, and payment requests outside of formal invoicing.

Romance

Romance Scams

Romance scams in Miami range from long-running online relationships that escalate to financial requests, to in-person manipulations where someone feigns romantic interest to extract money or favors. Miami's dating app scene and nightlife mix make short-term manipulation particularly easy.

Documented local patterns include faking pregnancies to extort cash payments and scamming men into buying flights or travel under the pretense of a relationship.

Red flags: New partners who quickly escalate emotional intensity, requests for money framed as urgent emergencies, claims of pregnancy or personal crisis shortly after meeting, and individuals who disappear after receiving payment.

Business & Investment

Business & Investment Fraud

Miami attracts entrepreneurs and investors from around the world — and scammers who prey on them. Business fraud ranges from outright contract breaches to real estate misrepresentation to Ponzi-style investment schemes. Victims often lose thousands before realizing the person they were dealing with had a documented history of fraud.

Red flags: Promised returns that seem unusually high, contractors or service providers who request full payment upfront, individuals with no verifiable business history or references, and anyone pressuring you to decide quickly.

Contractors

Contractor Scams

After major storms and during Miami's constant construction boom, unlicensed contractors and fraudulent renovation companies proliferate. They collect large upfront payments, do minimal or shoddy work, then disappear. Victims are often left with half-finished projects, legal liability, and no recourse.

Red flags: Contractors who can't produce a Florida license number, anyone asking for more than 10% upfront on a large job, door-to-door solicitations after storms, and vague or unwritten estimates.

Verify: Always look up contractors at myfloridalicense.com before signing anything. A licensed contractor has a verifiable registration number.

Online Services

Online Service Fraud

Freelancers and online service providers who collect payment for digital work — websites, marketing, publishing, social media management — and then deliver nothing (or low-quality AI-generated filler) are increasingly common in Miami. Victims often pay thousands before realizing the work will never be completed.

Red flags: Service providers with no verifiable portfolio, refusal to sign contracts with deliverables and timelines, repeated excuses for missing deadlines, and individuals who change their business name frequently.
